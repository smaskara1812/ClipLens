6. How AI minutes are counted

Because "AI minutes" is the resource we meter, we need to be precise about what it means.

An AI minute is one minute of wall-clock time spent by a Celery background worker running an AI processing task on your media. We measure the time from when the task starts on a worker to when it finishes or fails, and we log that elapsed time to your account.

Which tasks count

The following Celery tasks consume AI minutes:

  • Video transcoding to HLS (process_video_task)
  • Seek-preview sprite generation (generate_seek_thumbnails_task)
  • Audio track extraction (extract_audio_tracks_task)
  • Speech transcription with Whisper (generate_captions_task)
  • Subtitle translation with NLLB-200 (translate_subtitles_task)
  • Frame analysis: YOLO + CLIP + InsightFace + BLIP (analyze_video_frames_task)
  • Photo analysis (analyze_photo_task)
  • Speaker diarization with pyannote (run_diarization_task)
  • Audio-event detection with PANNs (detect_audio_events_task)
  • AI-generated video summaries (generate_video_summary_task)
  • Manual upscaling (upscale_video_task, upscale_photo_task)

Tasks that do not consume AI minutes: subtitle re-indexing (just VTT parsing), live-stream recording, and any pure database operations.

Why minutes (and not, say, "videos processed")

A 5-minute clip and a 5-hour film need wildly different amounts of compute. Per-minute pricing reflects what you actually consume, scales naturally with content length, and doesn't penalise short clips or reward batching long ones together.

Hardware dependence (important)

Wall-clock processing time depends on the hardware the worker runs on. A 10-minute video may take 4 AI minutes on a high-end GPU server, or 25 AI minutes on a CPU-only server. We control hardware for hosted deployments and publish a typical processing-time table on the billing page; for self-hosted deployments, your hardware determines your throughput.

For hosted plans on cliplens.com:

  • We commit to a baseline hardware tier per plan and publish expected processing speeds (see Plans).
  • If we materially change the hardware on which your media runs in a way that increases your AI minute consumption, we will notify you and adjust your monthly allowance accordingly.
  • We do not bill you for compute time spent on internal retries or our own infrastructure failures.

What gets logged on failure or retry

If an AI task fails partway through, we still log the elapsed time, because the compute was consumed. We cap automatic retries at a small bounded number per task — runaway loops cannot drain your account. User-facing errors (unsupported file format, missing audio track, etc.) fail early before consuming significant minutes.

Self-hosted deployments

When you run ClipLens on your own infrastructure under an enterprise self-host licence, the AI minute meter still runs (for your own observability), but no quotas are enforced and no usage is reported to us. Your licence fee covers the platform itself, not the compute — your hardware costs are your own.
